# Client brand and messaging guideline (test copy)

Client supplies process control and safety systems to refineries, petrochemical plants and gas processors in Southeast Asia. Our customers are engineers and plant leaders who value specifics over slogans.

## Words to avoid
- Never use: synergy, world-class, game-changing, revolutionary
- Avoid: best-in-class, cutting-edge
- Competitors: Rivalco, Acmetrol
- Do not mention Northstar Controls

## Voice
- Tone: formal
- Never open with: Just checking in, I hope this email finds you well
- No exclamation marks.

## Length
- Keep emails under 110 words.
- Keep sentences under 22 words.

## Sign-off
- Always sign off as: Client Process Automation Team

## Writing guidance
- Use British English spelling (modernise, programme, organisation).
- Name the customer's site or unit when the signal gives it, never just the company.
- Never promise a delivery date, a price or a saving.
- Write for an engineer: name the system or the unit, not "your operations".

## Messaging by situation
### Inquiry
- Angle: Answer the question they asked while it is still live, and offer a specialist.
- Likely objection: "We are only gathering information for now."
- Answer: A short call now saves a round of back-and-forth later, with no commitment.

### Modernisation
- Angle: Plan the move around one shutdown so the order of work is settled early.
- Likely objection: "The current system still works."
- Answer: It works today; spares and support end on a known date, so planning now keeps the choice yours.

### Service renewal
- Angle: Check the cover still matches how the site runs before renewing.
- Likely objection: "We just renew what we had."
- Answer: A short review often finds cover the site no longer needs and gaps it now has.

### Project
- Angle: Fix the control scope at front-end design, before it is expensive to change.
- Likely objection: "It is too early to talk about control systems."
- Answer: Front-end design is when control decisions are cheapest to make.

### Leadership
- Angle: Offer something useful for a new leader's first months, then a light ask.
- Likely objection: "I am still finding my feet."
- Answer: That is why we keep it short: one page on what peers prioritised first.

### Engagement
- Angle: Build on the topic they engaged with and relate it to their site.
- Likely objection: "I was just browsing."
- Answer: Then one practical example from a comparable site may be useful, nothing more.

## Messaging frameworks
### Book a first meeting: Trigger, insight, ask (Often)
1. Their trigger: the signal that makes it timely
2. One insight or one approved claim
3. One clear ask
Ask the reader to: Book a meeting; reply

### Answer an inquiry: Question, answer, next step (Often)
1. Restate what they asked
2. Answer it in two sentences
3. Offer a short call with a specialist
Ask the reader to: Request a call

### Start a migration conversation: Problem, cost of waiting, approach (Sometimes)
1. The problem they face
2. What waiting costs them
3. The approach that solves it
Ask the reader to: Book a meeting; read a guide

### Introduce ourselves to a new leader: Give, then ask (Sometimes)
1. Something useful for their first months
2. A light ask
Ask the reader to: Read content; book a meeting
