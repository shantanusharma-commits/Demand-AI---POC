What the AI sales platforms that are winning actually do

A read of 24 AI-native and incumbent sales platforms, their customer stories, the signals they use, the results they promise and what they charge. It is written for one decision: what Demand AI should build next for new-logo acquisition in long-cycle, high-ticket markets.

1
Signals, not lists, start the work

Every leader turns an event (a hire, a renewal date, a site visit, a new leader) into a play for a set persona. Single signals are weak; Unify's own illustrative data shows website visits alone give 1.9 meetings per 100 contacts against 11.2 for a new leader plus hiring.

2
First-party context beats bought data

Nooks mines its own call recordings for competitor contract end dates. Lightfield writes messages from your customers' own words. Warmly, Rox and Monaco store every action and its outcome. Everyone can buy the same third-party data.

3
Warm beats cold, by a lot

Kargo reports about 40% of warm calls book a meeting against 1% of cold calls (Warmly story). Lightfield maps the sales team's LinkedIn graph to find mutual-connection introductions.

4
Money follows the system of record

The best-funded and fastest-growing startups (Rox, Monaco, Reevo, Nooks, Lightfield, Unify) own the record and the workflow. Single-signal tools (Warmly, Common Room) were bought by HubSpot and Zoom.

5
Nobody charges per meeting or on revenue

Pricing has moved from seats to credits and agent actions. The closest to outcome pricing is HubSpot at $1 per lead worked and Leadsforge charging only for verified contacts. That is an open position.

6
Proof is short, small and self-reported

Most customer results cover weeks, not quarters, and rarely state a baseline. The strongest proof formats are a stated period, a baseline, a counterfactual and the customer's own CRM as referee.
