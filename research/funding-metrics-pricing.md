### Shared patterns (top group vs the rest)

1. **They own (or become) the system of record, built from captured activity, so agents have context.** Lightfield, Reevo, Clarify, Aurasell and Monaco build the CRM from email, calendar and calls; Rox sits on the warehouse and CRM as "a single source of truth". Evidence: a16z's "compounding context moat"; Kleiner's "system of friction"; Sequoia's "single source of truth". Contrast: Warmly (website visitor ID) and Common Room (signals layer) sat beside someone else's CRM and were bought by CRM or platform owners (HubSpot, Zoom). Inference: investors now pay for the record plus agents, not a feed into it.
2. **Consolidation is the headline promise, with explicit lists of tools replaced.** Reevo "four or five separate tools", Aurasell "15+ tools", Lightfield "one bill instead of six", Monaco replaces "traditional CRMs and disconnected point solutions" ([pricing](https://lightfield.com/pricing); [Aurasell](https://www.aurasell.ai/); [Monaco release](https://www.vcaonline.com/news/2026051211/monaco-secures-50-million-in-series-b-funding-to-grow-ai-powered-sales-platform-amid-strong-customer-demand/)). Battery: "Sales teams want consolidation." Smaller players (nRev, AiSDR) use the same pitch but against cheaper SMB stacks.
3. **Operator founders who lived the GTM pain, and investors say so.** Monaco (Sam Blond, ex-Brex CRO), Rox (Ishan Mukherjee, ex-New Relic growth lead), Reevo (DoorDash operators), Unify (Ramp growth product), Aurasell (ex-Harness CRO), Lightfield (Tome founders who "pivoted"). Each investor post leads on this ([Benchmark quote](https://www.vcaonline.com/news/2026051211/monaco-secures-50-million-in-series-b-funding-to-grow-ai-powered-sales-platform-amid-strong-customer-demand/); [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/partnering-with-rox-every-seller-needs-an-agent-swarm); [Kleiner](https://www.kleinerperkins.com/perspectives/reevo-the-intelligent-revenue-operating-system/); [Battery](https://www.battery.com/blog/outbound-is-broken-and-why-were-excited-about-unifying-the-sales-workflow/)). Contrast: kōdlo, nRev and Nex have no comparable public signal and raised little.
4. **Signal-timed, warm outbound instead of volume.** Unify (signals, "73% more" replies vendor claim), Rox (event-driven ranking), Nooks (one in three Pendo meetings from AI signals), Clarify buying Seam for signals. Battery's thesis is exactly this ("right message to the right person at the right time"). Contrast: AiSDR and Leadsforge sell volume per contact or per verified email and are thinly funded.
5. **Fast, visible time to value.** Kleiner on Nooks: productivity doubles "in the first two weeks"; Monaco: "impact within days"; Lightfield "builds itself... in minutes" (a16z); Aurasell "under two hours" (vendor). Investors quote speed-to-value more than long-run ROI.
6. **Agents do the work but humans approve customer-facing actions.** Reevo drafts actions for reps to approve; Nooks calls its tools "assistants" and Kleiner backs "supercharging human SDRs"; Rox and Lightfield keep reps in the loop (base research). Contrast: fully autonomous AI SDR pitches (AiSDR) have smaller funding. Inference: investors back "copilot plus automation" over "replace the SDR" in this cohort.
7. **Pricing on work done, not seats.** Monaco (credits, no seats), Rox (Agent Actions), Lightfield (workspace plus pooled credits), Clarify (unlimited seats, credits). a16z cites it explicitly as a reason to invest. Exception: Nooks is per seat yet among the strongest, because it started as a rep productivity tool.
8. **Software plus forward-deployed humans.** Monaco includes a forward-deployed AE; Rox and Reevo hire forward-deployed engineers; Lightfield's Growth tier includes a GTM engineer (base research; [Monaco pricing](https://www.monaco.com/pricing); [Lightfield pricing](https://lightfield.com/pricing)). Inference: early results are partly service-delivered, which helps the fast revenue ramps investors reward. nRev does the same at small scale.
9. **Selling into the new generation of AI-native, fast-growing companies first.** Unify (Perplexity, Cursor, Together AI), Monaco ("the most promising startups"), Lightfield ("fastest growing companies"), Clarify (early-stage teams). a16z: the AI-native generation is "choosing its system of record for the first time". Contrast: Rox goes the other way (enterprise account-based selling) and still ranks first, so enterprise is a valid but harder path.
10. **Budget capture beyond software: going after headcount.** Battery says agents capture "headcount budgets"; Monaco's "3-person team running GTM like a 20-person sales org"; Unify's Perplexity case ("without a single BDR") ([Unify](https://www.unifygtm.com/blog/how-perplexity-booked-1-7m-in-pipeline-without-a-single-bdr)).
11. **They buy capability and ship fast.** Reevo bought Ciro, Clarify bought Seam, Nooks bought FullyRamped; Unify shipped 187 features in 2025 (vendor). Contrast: the acquired companies (Warmly, Common Room) were the capability being bought.
12. **Weaker or acquired players share narrower wedges.** Single-signal tools (Warmly: website visitors; Common Room: community and product signals), single-motion tools (Conversica: lead follow-up, now PE-owned and pivoting to car dealers) and low-cost self-serve volume tools (AiSDR, Leadsforge, nRev) either sold, recapitalised or stayed small. Leadsforge/Salesforge is the exception on efficiency (claimed $3M ARR on $500K and profitable), showing a capital-light path for SMB tools, but not one VCs are funding at scale.

## Pricing patterns

- **Nobody in this set prices on booked meetings or revenue.** The closest are "pay for verified results" (Leadsforge charges only for a verified email or phone; Clarify only for a successful AI field fill) and HubSpot's per-lead agent charge ($1 per lead). Rox's footer explicitly disclaims guaranteed outcomes. Inference: outcome pricing in the strict sense (per meeting, per opportunity) is an open position.
- **AI-native startups meter agent work in credits or actions; seats are disappearing at the top of the startup market.** Monaco (no seat fee, $0.01 per credit), Rox (Agent Actions), Clarify (unlimited seats), Lightfield (workspace price plus pooled credits). a16z names this as a reason to invest. Seat-only pricing survives at Nooks (custom quote) and kōdlo.
- **Incumbents are hybrid: seats plus credits, moving towards credits.** Salesforce (per user plus Flex Credits, about $0.10 per action), HubSpot (seats plus credits, $10 per 1,000), Outreach (tiers defined by credit allowances), ZoomInfo (shifting to Data and AI Credits and Records Under Management), Apollo (seat plus credits, freemium).
- **What is metered:** AI actions or agent tasks (Rox, Monaco, Outreach, Salesforce), enrichment of contacts (Leadsforge, ZoomInfo, Apollo), people contacted (AiSDR $1.50 each), leads worked (HubSpot $1 each), active contacts in sequence (Agent Frank), records under management (ZoomInfo), and AI research per record (Clarify 3 or 30 credits). Search itself is often free (Leadsforge, ZoomInfo GTM.AI).
- **Entry price bands:** free tiers at Unify, kōdlo, Apollo, Leadsforge (100 credits), nRev (2,500 credits); self-serve paid entry clusters at $20 to $100 a month (Unify $20/seat, HubSpot Starter $7 to $20/seat, Apollo $49, Leadsforge $49, Nex $49, kōdlo $49, Rox $100); mid-market platforms jump to about $1,000 to $2,500 a month (Lightfield Pro $999 and Growth $1,999, nRev $999 to $2,499, Conversica about $2,999 third-party); enterprise and data tools run $10,000 to $30,000+ a year (Monaco $10,000 upfront, Warmly about $10,000, Aurasell about $20,000, Common Room about $27,000 to $30,000, Bombora about $30,000, all third-party except Monaco).
- **Human services are bundled into higher tiers as part of the price.** Monaco includes a forward-deployed AE; Lightfield Growth includes a GTM engineer and Enterprise a forward-deployed engineer; nRev Scale and Done-for-you include 5 and 20 human hours a month; AiSDR sells a $149 managed campaign and $2,500/month fully managed service; Outreach offers forward-deployed engineers. Inference: buyers are paying for outcomes indirectly through service, not through outcome-based contracts.
- **Spend controls are now a selling point.** Hard caps and visible usage logs appear at Lightfield (credit hard caps), Rox (activity log, tunable Insights), Monaco (live usage breakdown) and HubSpot (account caps up to 50% above monthly credits, action-level caps, from 15 October 2026). Inference: buyers worry about unpredictable agent bills, so predictability is part of the offer.
- **Many leaders still hide prices.** Reevo, Nooks, Aurasell, Warmly (since the HubSpot deal), Common Room, Conversica, 6sense, Bombora, Outreach and Salesloft publish no price list; third-party estimates fill the gap. Transparent self-serve pricing is mainly a feature of smaller or PLG-led players (Unify, Rox, Lightfield, Leadsforge, AiSDR, nRev, kōdlo).
