### Rox: why customers like it (themes, with story counts)

Typical before-state: 20 to 25 minutes per email (LogicMonitor), 45 minutes per call prep (New Relic), 4 to 8 hours per client question (WSP).
2. **It tells them which accounts and people matter now (8 of 19).** Upwind (ranked accounts fed in), Rev.io ("not on our radar"), Together AI ("highest-priority accounts at the right moment"), Ramp ("No clear system for prioritization"), WSP (which clients were hit by weather), Codat ("the 100 that matter most"), Axon 911 ("which 20 to focus on"), Rho ("they don't know who to call").
3. **One place, kept current without data entry (9 of 19).** XBOW (12 tools to 5, live source of truth), Upwind (six tools to one), Cloud Software Group (scattered data), Pallet (CEO Mode roll-up), Ramp ("No extra logins"), Rho (self-updating records), New Relic (one dashboard), Puma Energy (internal plus external in one place), Snorkel (consolidation).
4. **Personalisation that buyers notice (7 of 19).** Axon 911 (Facebook post), LogicMonitor ("really sounds hyper personalized", 70%+ opens), Rev.io ("The single hardest thing is the personalization"), MongoDB ("technical, brand-aligned and region-aware"), Together AI ("technically sophisticated buyers"), Tabs (2 to 3x open and response), Snorkel (messaging "resonated").
5. **More coverage without more headcount (7 of 19).** Rev.io (TAM 5x, same team), LogicMonitor (all 300 accounts), Tabs ("do more with less"), Axon 911 ("balance everything on their plate"), Pallet (5 to 40 reps without losing control), Together AI ("far more customers"), Bynder.
6. **Prepared for meetings and warned about deal risk (7 of 19).** Pallet (competitor evaluation, weak champion), Ramp, New Relic, Couchbase (calendar briefs), Axon 911 (Outlook prep), Rho ("hyper prepared"), Cloud Software Group (renewal decisions).
7. **The vendor behaves like a building partner (8 of 19).** XBOW ("thinking six to twelve months ahead"), Upwind (feature "launched within the next week"), Codat (org chart shipped in two weeks), Together AI (forward-deployed team), Bynder (role-by-role design), Couchbase (seven role-based sessions), WSP ("responsiveness of the Rox team"), Cloud Software Group ("They understand how the market works").
8. **Reps actually adopt it (8 of 19).** Couchbase (90%+), Puma Energy (2% to 80 to 90%), Bynder (100+ MAU in 90 days), WSP (600 users), Ramp ("I've told everyone on my team to do the same"), New Relic (CRM-native), XBOW (sellers asking for new integrations), Axon 911 (skipped rest of pilot).

Hard numbers are scarce: only 7 of 19 stories give a quantified outcome tied to revenue activity (XBOW, Rev.io, LogicMonitor, Axon 911, Bynder, New Relic, Tabs); the rest give time saved, adoption or no number.

### Rox: what is unique in how they deliver results

- **An agent per account that keeps researching, not a list that goes stale.** Rev.io's "dedicated agent for every account", Codat's "monitors Codat's book of accounts 24/7", Rho's agent "consistently updating every contact, every record". Inference: the unit of work is the account, which suits named-account selling.
- **Unusual, real-world trigger sources turned into a reason to call.** Legislation, local news, community publications and Facebook posts (Axon 911), extreme weather incidents (WSP), 10-K reports (LogicMonitor). The question is asked in plain language over a named list (WSP: "let's load our top 30 to 40 clients and just ask it").
- **Writing tuned on the customer's own past emails and brand constraints.** LogicMonitor: "how it ingests emails we've sent in the past"; MongoDB: "technical, brand-aligned and region-aware".
- **Outputs pushed into the moment of use.** Calendar-linked pre-meeting briefs (Couchbase, Axon 911), CEO Mode roll-up with risk flags (Pallet), registration list to sequence after an event (Axon 911), CRM fields filled automatically (Puma Energy, Snorkel).
- **Forward-deployed delivery and proof on the buyer's own accounts.** POC on Rev.io's "actual target prospects"; XBOW bake-off measured on meetings; features shipped in one to two weeks (Upwind, Codat); hands-on training (Together AI, Couchbase, Bynder).

### Rox: what Demand AI can adopt

- **Prospecting and Qualified lead identification: an always-on research agent per named account that watches industrial trigger events.** Examples to configure for the pilot client: plant expansions and capex announcements, new permits, regulatory changes, tenders, incidents and outages, annual report statements (modelled on WSP, Axon 911 and LogicMonitor). Output: "why this account, why now" in one line, with the source link.
- **Segmentation and Lead scoring: rank the territory to the "20 of 100" that matter, and map the buying committee with confidence levels.** Codat's org chart with confidence on reporting lines and Axon 911's 20-of-100 framing fit industrial accounts where the plant manager, engineering, procurement and finance all matter.
- **Content creation: draft in the pilot client's voice by learning from their past sent emails and technical documents, with brand and regional rules.** LogicMonitor and MongoDB show this is what makes buyers reply and what large IT teams approve.
- **NBA and NBC, and Deal: deal-risk flags drawn from emails, calls and notes.** Pallet's two flags (buyer assessing an alternative; champion without authority) are exactly the risks in long industrial cycles. Pair with a calendar-triggered pre-meeting brief (Couchbase, Axon 911).
- **Follow-up and response tracking: trade show and event follow-up.** Upload the visitor or registration list, auto-build account histories before the event and run personalised follow-up afterwards (Axon 911: "Those leads just used to get left by the wayside"). Delivery lesson: run the pilot on the client's real target list and judge it on meetings, as XBOW and Rev.io did.

### Warmly: what is unique in how they deliver results

- **Person-level identification joined to a live moment.** A Slack alert with name, company, pages and session count while the person is still on the site, and the ability to "warm call" into the live session by chat or video (Namecoach 2023, Kandji, Warmly sales). Inference: the product sells the moment, not the list.
- **AI chat with designed human takeover.** AI opens with a company-aware line, a human is alerted when the visitor replies, and some customers set an AI delay so humans go first (Kandji transcripts, Zinc Work "30 seconds").
- **Many signals into one CRM route.** Visits, Bombora research, hiring, social engagers and competitor-post engagers all land in HubSpot or Salesforce through one naming convention, one sequence and one human task at the qualifier (TeamWeave, Innerspace).
- **Closing the loop between outbound and the website.** Unique links or query parameters in outbound emails identify who clicked and what they read next (Behavioral Signals, Warmly sales, TotalSDS sequence attribution, StraightIn UTMs).
- **A services layer for teams without SDRs.** WarmLegency places trained SDRs who run the stack (Namecoach, Dyspatch, Alpharun), and onboarding staff sit in the customer's Slack (Caddis, Behavioral Signals).

### Warmly: what Demand AI can adopt

- **Qualified lead identification and Lead scoring: an account engagement score built from page type, number of distinct people and dwell time, written into the CRM view.** Use Warmly's own published rule (pricing or case study pages, several visitors, over five active minutes in a week; https://www.warmly.ai/p/case-studies/warmly-marketing) adapted to industrial pages: product specs, datasheets, CAD downloads, request-for-quote, service and spares. Add a "last seen within 30 days" field to account lists (Zinc Work).
- **Deal and NBA: alert when new people from an account in an open deal start reading.** TotalSDS's champion-sharing signal maps directly to an industrial buying committee widening (engineering to procurement to finance). Next best action: offer the champion a business-case pack or a site visit.
- **Follow-up and response tracking: tracked links in every outbound email, plus dormant-account reactivation.** Tie a click and later visits to the sequence (Behavioral Signals, TotalSDS), and flag accounts that return after months of silence (Inbox Pirates: "if they come back, say, six months later").
- **Campaign execution and Content mapping: match follow-up to the campaign and content that brought the visitor.** Caddis (manufacturing software) sends messaging that mirrors the AdWords landing page; Subskribe reshapes material by pages viewed; HG Insights and UTTR ungate content and treat engagement as the signal.
- **Prospecting and NBC: competitor-audience lists and the honest soft opener.** Pull engagers of competitor and trade-body posts (Innerspace), and use a non-salesy channel and message such as "somebody from your company was on our website ... No pitch" (Aye Aye Agency) or "Noticed you're interested in ..." (Monograph). Inference: AI chat and live video are low priority for the pilot client, whose site traffic is likely too low for chat to matter.

## Side by side (Inference)

- Rox wins on **depth per named account**: research, buying-committee mapping, trigger events from the outside world and deal-risk flags. Its strongest industrial analogues are WSP, Axon 911, Pallet, Codat, LogicMonitor and Puma Energy.
- Warmly wins on **timing from first-party behaviour**: who from which account is reading what, right now. Its strongest industrial analogues are Caddis, TeamWeave, Kargo, TotalSDS, Zinc Work outbound and the Aye Aye Agency utility example.
- For a long-cycle industrial seller, the combination that matters is Rox-style outside-in triggers to pick accounts and craft the reason to call, plus Warmly-style inside-out engagement signals (multi-person visits, return after silence, tracked links) to time the next action. Both vendors' customers praise the same things in the end: less research, a clear "who and why now", and messages that show the seller did their homework.
