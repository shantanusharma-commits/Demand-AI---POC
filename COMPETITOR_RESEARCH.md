# Competitor research: AI sales and GTM platforms

Prepared for Demand AI (Next Best Campaign). Research date: 27 October 2026.

## How to read this document

- **Scope.** 27 entries: 19 AI-native platforms (Group A) and 8 established players (Group B). Entries 4 and 16 turned out to be the same product (Leadsforge), and Baze could not be identified, so there are 25 researched companies plus one identification note.
- **Sources.** Each company section lists the source types reviewed. First-party sources come first. Third-party coverage is used only to verify or add context and is marked "(third-party)". Every claim carries an inline link.
- **Labels.** "Vendor claim" marks a figure the company states about itself that we could not verify. "Not published" means the company does not disclose the detail. "Inference:" marks our own reasoning, which should not be read as fact.
- **Limits of this pass.** Direct page fetching was blocked by the network proxy for most vendor domains, and the scraping tool was rate-limited. Where a first-party page could not be opened in full, the evidence comes from search extracts of that page, cited to the first-party URL and flagged in the batch notes. A small number of secondary pages (some docs and changelogs) were not reached; each section says so where it matters.
- **"What I liked about their approach"** is left blank in every section for you to complete.
- Each company section uses the same seven headings. Each "What we can learn and adopt" bullet starts with the Demand AI stage it applies to: Prospecting, Qualified lead identification, Lead scoring, Segmentation, NBA and NBC, Campaign execution, Content creation, Content mapping, Follow-up and response tracking, Deal.

### 1. Clarify (with Seam AI)
**Website:** [clarify.ai](https://www.clarify.ai) and [getseam.ai](https://www.getseam.ai/) | **Founded / HQ:** Clarify early 2024, Seattle (plus a San Francisco office since the Seam deal); Seam 2020, San Francisco ([GeekWire, third-party](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/seattles-clarify-acquires-s-f-startup-seam-ai-joining-forces-to-challenge-crm-stalwarts/)) | **Category:** AI-native "autonomous" CRM, adding signals and account-based marketing (ABM) through the Seam acquisition | **Sources checked:** site, pricing, agents page, customer case study (Tesorio), company blog (acquisition post), Seam homepage and blog, Seam docs (search excerpt), funding and M&A press, AWS Marketplace listing

#### 1. Why they are performing well
**Verified facts**
- Clarify raised a $15m Series A in June 2025, bringing total funding to $22.5m from U.S. Venture Partners, Gradient Ventures and Madrona ([Clarify blog, 25 June 2025](https://www.clarify.ai/blog/series-a); [GeekWire, third-party](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/seattles-clarify-acquires-s-f-startup-seam-ai-joining-forces-to-challenge-crm-stalwarts/)).
- The founders previously built Iteratively, which Amplitude acquired in 2021 ([GeekWire, third-party](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/seattles-clarify-acquires-s-f-startup-seam-ai-joining-forces-to-challenge-crm-stalwarts/)).
- **The deal (21 July 2026):** Clarify acquired Seam AI, its first acquisition. Terms were not disclosed. Five Seam staff joined, including co-founder and CEO Nicholas Scavone, which took Clarify to 30 people. Clarify says it raised additional (undisclosed) funding as part of the deal and is considering a Series B in early 2027 ([GeekWire, third-party](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/seattles-clarify-acquires-s-f-startup-seam-ai-joining-forces-to-challenge-crm-stalwarts/); [Clarify blog](https://www.clarify.ai/blog/welcoming-seam-ai-to-clarify)).
- Seam's homepage now reads "Seam AI is joining Clarify to build the CRM that generates revenue, not just manages it" and points visitors to the Clarify Signals waitlist ([getseam.ai](https://www.getseam.ai/)).
- **What Seam brings:** about $7m raised (angel funding plus a Bessemer-led seed in April 2024) and customers including Zapier, GoFundMe, Drata and Betterment ([GeekWire, third-party](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/seattles-clarify-acquires-s-f-startup-seam-ai-joining-forces-to-challenge-crm-stalwarts/); [Clarify blog](https://www.clarify.ai/blog/welcoming-seam-ai-to-clarify)). Seam customers are on hold while the technology is folded into Clarify.
- Named case study: Tesorio moved off a ten-year-old Salesforce instance in under a month and replaced its ICP scoring tool (Keyplay), a call recorder and its outbound stack ([Clarify case study, 30 April 2026](https://www.clarify.ai/customers/tesorio)).

**Vendor claims**
- "Trusted by 8,000+ GTM teams" ([homepage](https://www.clarify.ai)).
- Tesorio: "$50,000+/yr in tools cut", about five hours saved per deal, and Q1 net-new pipeline goal beaten by 34%. Defensive Networks: "98% less CRM data entry", about 120 hours a week of manual entry removed ([homepage](https://www.clarify.ai); [Tesorio](https://www.clarify.ai/customers/tesorio)).
- Seam Agent Plays: "4x faster signal-to-outreach" and a "35% increase in qualified pipeline" for early adopters ([Seam blog](https://www.getseam.ai/blog/introducing-seam-s-agent-plays)).

#### 2. How they promise results
- Clarify promises to remove CRM admin ("Selling is human. Clarify runs the rest"). Outcomes are framed as tool consolidation savings, hours saved and less data entry ([homepage](https://www.clarify.ai)).
- **Pricing is tied to work done, not seats.** Every plan has unlimited seats. Credits pay for AI work: for example, 30 credits per meeting summary and 1 per campaign email. Plans are Free (1k credits a month), Starter (from $40 a month billed annually, 5k to 40k credits), Growth (from $1,000 a month, 100k+ credits) and Enterprise (custom). Enrichment and meeting recording are free and never use credits ([pricing](https://www.clarify.ai/pricing)). A third-party AWS listing shows a 50,000-credit, 12-month contract at $500 ([AWS Marketplace, third-party](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-vkrkvx5hvhabc)).
- Seam framed its outcomes around pipeline: timing outreach to "intent spikes" ([Seam blog](https://www.getseam.ai/blog/introducing-seam-s-agent-plays)).

#### 3. How they generate their outputs
- **Data in:** Clarify builds the CRM automatically from connected email and calendar, including past history. It records calls "with or without a bot", then summarises them and updates records. It also has a built-in prospect database of more than 28m companies and 175m people, plus live web search ([homepage](https://www.clarify.ai); [agents FAQ](https://www.clarify.ai/agents)).
- **Prospecting:** "Clarify watches for buying signals (hiring, funding, job changes, etc.) then adds the right prospects to your CRM, already enriched." Lead Finder turns a plain-English request into a saved search. "Email score" confidence appears per contact ([homepage](https://www.clarify.ai)).
- **Scoring and segmentation:** "AI Fields" score inbound leads, tier accounts by ICP fit and segment leads by target market (Tesorio, FutureFit AI) ([Tesorio](https://www.clarify.ai/customers/tesorio); [homepage](https://www.clarify.ai)).
- **Execution:** the agent drafts multi-step campaigns across LinkedIn and email (connection request, then email, then threaded follow-up) and drafts replies "in your voice". Campaigns are shown for review before sending ([homepage](https://www.clarify.ai)).
- **Agents:** each agent has a trigger, instructions and scoped permissions (can read, write, reach, learn). Templates include Post-meeting CRM Update, Deal Risk Radar and Account Research System, which writes a SPICED discovery brief ([agents](https://www.clarify.ai/agents)).
- **Seam's flow:** monitor signals continuously, pick best-fit personas when an event fires, then push contacts into the CRM, the sales engagement tool, marketing automation and ad channels. Plays include website visitors, LinkedIn ad engagement, re-engaging closed-lost deals, new hires and new executives ([Seam blog](https://www.getseam.ai/blog/introducing-seam-s-agent-plays)).

#### 4. Technical mechanisms and interesting approaches
- **One record graph inside and outside the company.** In Clarify's words, the CRM "understands everything happening inside your business" and Seam "understood everything happening outside it". Clarify Signals merges first- and third-party signals and makes them native CRM context for agents ([Clarify blog](https://www.clarify.ai/blog/welcoming-seam-ai-to-clarify)). Thompson said Seam's value is data "not necessarily easy to get from the web" ([GeekWire, third-party](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/seattles-clarify-acquires-s-f-startup-seam-ai-joining-forces-to-challenge-crm-stalwarts/)).
- **Agents with teammate-style permissions** that "learn from your edits and feedback", log every action, and use Skills (reusable plain-language instructions) plus custom MCP connectors ([agents](https://www.clarify.ai/agents)).
- **MCP-first distribution:** there is an official MCP server, listed in the Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor and Slack marketplaces, and early access in iMessage and email ([homepage](https://www.clarify.ai)).
- **Fine-grained credit metering per AI action**, with per-feature toggles and spend caps ([pricing](https://www.clarify.ai/pricing)).
- Models used, signal-scoring method and the identity resolution behind Seam's website-visitor plays: Not published.

#### 5. Why they get the results they get
- **Workflow fit:** capture is automatic (inbox, calendar, calls), so the CRM fills itself. This removes the main reason CRMs fail.
- **Consolidation economics:** customers justify switching by cancelling three or more tools. Seat-free pricing removes the cost of adding users.
- **Speed to value:** a free plan, setup from the inbox, and migration support (30 days for Tesorio).
- **Signals as a moat:** buying Seam gives Clarify harder-to-get data and an ABM customer base, and puts it up against 6sense and Demandbase.
- Inference: the 8,000+ team figure is mostly free and small-team usage. Revenue and ARR are not published.

#### 6. What we can learn and adopt
- **Prospecting:** treat signals (funding, hiring, job moves, web visits) as automatic triggers that add enriched prospects to the account list, not as a separate feed.
- **Lead scoring:** add LLM "AI fields" that score fit against a written ICP in plain language and show the reason, alongside our rules-based score.
- **Segmentation:** let users segment by an AI-classified field (for example, target market), as FutureFit does.
- **Next Best Action (NBA) and Next Best Campaign (NBC):** copy Seam's "Agent Plays" format: named, pre-built plays (website visitor, closed-lost re-engage, new executive) that each have a trigger, a persona rule and a channel mix. This maps directly onto NBC recommendations.
- **Campaign execution:** show the generated multi-step LinkedIn and email sequence for one-click review, so it can be exported to Dripify or email tools.
- **Follow-up and response tracking:** draft post-meeting follow-ups grounded in the transcript. This is the most valued feature in Clarify's case study.
- **Deal:** attribute pipeline to the campaign or play that sourced it (Clarify's dinner-sourced pipeline report), so we can prove NBC impact.

#### 2. How they promise results
- Positioning: "Revenue should feel predictable." The promise is to replace the "four or five separate tools" teams pay for (CRM, data, enrichment, sequencer, dialler, call recorder) ([homepage](https://reevo.ai); [Ciro release](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/press_releases/reevo-strengthens-its-ai-powered-prospecting-and-outreach-with-acquisition-of-ciro/article_40518000-e84f-5e94-874d-38c3c0746d74.html)).

- Pricing: not published. Quote-based, reportedly in Core, Pro and Enterprise tiers ([Salesforge review, third-party competitor](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/reevo-ai-review); [Capterra, third-party](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1172503/Reevo)).

#### 3. How they generate their outputs
- **Find:** TAM sourcing from a built-in "database of millions of verified contacts", one-click enrichment (each email is verified against the prospect's company), live web research saved to the record with sources, automatic dedupe and mapping to existing accounts, qualification of web-form leads against screening criteria, and rule-based lead routing. Intent signals, lead scoring and lookalike "signal prospecting" are marked "Soon" ([Find](https://reevo.ai/products/find)).
- **Connect:** domain purchase, inbox warming, sequences and dialler, scheduler and reply tracking ([homepage](https://reevo.ai)).

#### 4. Technical mechanisms and interesting approaches
- **First-party activity capture as the data core.** Reevo generates its own activity data rather than syncing from third-party tools ([Business Wire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20251105759977/en/Reevo-Debuts-with-$80-Million-in-Funding-as-the-Only-AI-Native-GTM-Platform-Spanning-Marketing-Sales-and-Customer-Success)).
- **Ciro's search index:** a "multi-terabyte search index covering hundreds of millions of person and company profiles", with "multiple search passes that progressively refine precision as the system learns what a customer actually wants" ([Ciro release](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/press_releases/reevo-strengthens-its-ai-powered-prospecting-and-outreach-with-acquisition-of-ciro/article_40518000-e84f-5e94-874d-38c3c0746d74.html)). This is iterative retrieval with preference memory.
- **Closed loop from downstream to targeting:** "everything learned downstream feeds back into who our customers go after next" (Ciro CEO). The mechanism is Not published.
- **Graduated autonomy:** an approve, edit or dismiss queue, plus dry-runs for no-code workflows and field change history ([Foundation](https://reevo.ai/products/foundation)).
- Owned deliverability layer (domain purchase and warming inside the CRM).
- MCP to Notion, Linear, Pylon and PostHog. Security: SOC 2 and ISO 27001 ([RevGenius, third-party](https://www.revgenius.com/perks/reevo)).
- Models, scoring method and the forecasting approach: Not published.

#### 5. Why they get the results they get
- **Capital and distribution:** $80m and tier-one investors pay for a full-suite build and senior hires from Box, Salesforce and DoorDash. Khosla's managing director appears in the site testimonials.
- **Buying rather than building gaps:** Ciro brought a prospecting engine and an existing user base.
- **Wedge:** sales first, for small to mid-sized B2B teams leaving HubSpot. The "breath of fresh air" testimonial comes from a HubSpot switcher ([homepage](https://reevo.ai)).
- Inference: results so far are more about the funding story and the consolidation pitch than published outcomes. Lead scoring and intent are still "Soon".

#### 6. What we can learn and adopt
- **Qualified lead identification:** verify each email against the prospect's company domain before it enters the list, and auto-dedupe and map contacts to existing accounts. This extends our current validation step.
- **Lead scoring:** let users state 10 or more qualifying criteria per contact and keep them as remembered ICP and exclusion rules, as Ciro does.
- **Segmentation:** use closed-won lookalikes ("every win triggers a search for lookalike buyers") as an automatic micro-segment.
- **Next Best Action (NBA) and Next Best Campaign (NBC):** use a ranked "what changed overnight" feed with approve, edit or dismiss per action, and widen autonomy as acceptance rates rise.
- **Content mapping to each outreach sequence step:** ground every recommendation in cited evidence (a link to the signal or record), as Ask Reevo does.
- **Follow-up and response tracking:** log reply and meeting outcomes back against the segment so the next round of targeting learns from them.

### 3. nRev
**Website:** [nrev.ai](https://www.nrev.ai) | **Founded / HQ:** 2022 as Nurturev, by Sayanta Ghosh, Nikhil Ojha and Rajat Jain ([StartupIntros, third-party](https://startupintros.com/orgs/nurturev)); HQ Not published | **Category:** GTM automation platform ("AI GTM engineer"): signal-based workflows and playbooks for RevOps | **Sources checked:** site (home, playbooks, pricing, FAQs), customer stories (Mindtickle, PriceLabs), YouTube demo, Product Hunt listing (third-party tracker), directories

#### 1. Why they are performing well
**Verified facts**

- Has a public, self-serve pricing table and a SOC 2 Type II claim ([homepage](https://www.nrev.ai)).

Oloid: $10k saved upfront on tooling and 50+ high-confidence opportunities a week ([homepage](https://www.nrev.ai)).

#### 2. How they promise results
- The promise is tool replacement plus pipeline automation: "Ditch your 25-tool GTM stack." A comparison table names the tools each playbook replaces (UserGems, RB2B, HeyReach, Clay, ZoomInfo, n8n) and their prices ([homepage](https://www.nrev.ai)).

New workspaces get 2,500 free credits. Test Mode is free, and estimated credits are shown per node before a run ([homepage](https://www.nrev.ai)). An ROI calculator is offered.
- The top tiers bundle human GTM-engineer hours. Inference: part of the outcome is delivered as a service.

#### 3. How they generate their outputs
- **Playbooks:** "Steal Competitors' Deals" (monitor competitor sales reps' LinkedIn activity to find accounts evaluating them), "Signal Based Outbound" (funding, hiring, tech stack changes, regulation changes and "25 more"), "LinkedIn Growth Engine" (ICP engaging with your content or competitors' content), "Website Visitor Tracking" (reverse-IP and fingerprinting) and "New Pipeline from CRM Data" (champion moves, closed-won lookalikes, forgotten deals) ([homepage](https://www.nrev.ai)).
- **Flow (Mindtickle):** monitor ICP accounts, detect signals, validate persona and geography, enrich, generate summaries and talking points, then create and route leads in Salesforce ([case study](https://www.nrev.ai/customer-stories/mindtickle-fully-automated-pipeline-generation-inside-salesforce)).
- **Content (PriceLabs):** a weekly research job; then daily, it detects language, pulls relevant case studies and tone guidelines, and generates a 5-step sequence. A second AI "verifier" refines it. Every fourth sequence goes to a Slack bot for human review, and approved sequences go to Smartlead. Cohorts are tagged for A/B tests ([case study](https://www.nrev.ai/customer-stories/how-pricelabs-built-an-ai-powered-outbound-engine)).
- **Build modes:** describe the goal in plain English; the agent "asks the right questions, picks the right playbook" and builds an editable visual workflow. There is also a CLI for Claude Code ([homepage](https://www.nrev.ai)).

#### 4. Technical mechanisms and interesting approaches
- **Deterministic agents:** "AI is used where it adds value, not on every step". Execution paths are fixed, cost per play is predictable, runs are "100% idempotent", progress is checkpointed and retried, and every step is logged and reversible ([homepage](https://www.nrev.ai)). nRev contrasts this explicitly with open-ended agents such as Relevance AI.
- **"GTM Ontology" and "Knowledge House":** a domain data model so the AI "speaks your language", plus a map of the customer's existing tools. Managed integrations come with "preferential API limits with GTM vendors" ([FAQ](https://www.nrev.ai)). Details: Not published.
- **Generator plus verifier LLM pattern** with sampled human review (1 in 4) ([PriceLabs](https://www.nrev.ai/customer-stories/how-pricelabs-built-an-ai-powered-outbound-engine)).
- **Competitor-rep social listening** as an intent source (novel).
- Website de-anonymisation: reverse-IP plus fingerprinting (stated). Data vendors and match rates: Not published. Models: Not published.

#### 5. Why they get the results they get
- **Wedge:** RevOps and GTM engineers who would otherwise stitch together Clay, n8n and point tools. nRev sells packaged, proven plays rather than a blank canvas.
- **Services-assisted delivery:** case studies read like bespoke builds (Google Sheets as the data store at PriceLabs). Inference: results depend partly on nRev's team building the workflows.
- **Cost transparency:** credits are shown per node, Test Mode is free, and the published price is low compared with the stack it replaces.

#### 6. What we can learn and adopt
- **Prospecting:** add a "competitor engagement" signal (people engaging with competitor posts or reps) and a "champion moved" signal from CRM history.
- **Lead scoring:** score each signal against the ICP before it triggers anything ("filter out the noise").
- **Next Best Action (NBA) and Next Best Campaign (NBC):** package recommendations as named, deterministic playbooks with a visible step list and a cost estimate. This suits a rules-based prototype well.
- **Content creation:** use a generator plus verifier pass on drafts (tone, structure), with language detection for multi-market outreach.
- **Content mapping to each outreach sequence step:** pull case studies and tone guidelines per step from our content library, as PriceLabs does.
- **Follow-up and response tracking:** tag A/B cohorts at send time, and sample 1 in N drafts for human approval as a quality gate.

## Cross-company synthesis

Sources for every figure below are in the company sections. Section numbers are given in brackets.

### Common patterns

1. **Signals decide who and when, not lists.** Almost every AI-native player starts from a trigger (web visit, job change, funding, hiring, content engagement, inquiry) rather than a static list: Unify plays (6), Seam's Agent Plays inside Clarify (1), Common Room (13), Warmly (14), nRev (3), AiSDR (17). The incumbents sell the same idea through intent data: 6sense (23), Bombora (24), HubSpot Breeze Intelligence (27). Unify claims replies double once four or more distinct signals stack on one account (vendor claim, 6).
2. **Every recommendation comes with a reason.** HubSpot shows a "Reason to Reach Out" per account (27), Salesloft Rhythm explains each ranked action (26), 6sense shows which signals moved a score (23), Monaco writes "why this account" (10), Lightfield gives a single next step with cited evidence (7), and kōdlo cites every research claim (5). Demand AI's "why now" line is the right idea; the best players make the reason specific and traceable.
3. **Data is rented and stacked, not owned.** Most AI-native players aggregate providers through enrichment waterfalls: Leadsforge 20+ providers (4 and 16), Unify 40+ vendors (6), AiSDR 20 providers (17), Rox 15 providers (15), Common Room (13). Only the incumbents own a dataset at scale: ZoomInfo (21), Apollo's contributor network (20), Bombora's co-op (24), 6sense's intent network (23).
4. **Capture first, so the record writes itself.** The new CRMs build the account record from email, calendar and calls instead of manual entry: Clarify (1), Reevo (2), Lightfield (7), Rox (15), Monaco (10), Aurasell (18). That captured history is what lets them learn which signals lead to meetings.
5. **Deterministic workflow with AI pockets beats open-ended autonomy.** Salesforce's Agent Script mixes scripted steps with LLM steps (22), nRev sells deterministic, idempotent playbooks with a generator plus verifier model (3), and Workvivo's Journeys pairs a rules-based engine with AI drafting (11). This validates Demand AI's rules engine as the backbone.
6. **Human approval is a dial, not a switch.** Review-before-send is the default at HubSpot (27), Salesforce and Outreach let teams choose draft or autonomous per segment (22, 25), ZoomInfo and AiSDR use approval gates and per-play limits (21, 17), Nex gates every write (8), and kōdlo leaves sending to humans (5).
7. **Replies are handled inside the product.** Conversica works every lead with a persistence policy and stop rules and hands off on intent (12). AiSDR classifies replies and diagnoses whether the audience or the angle failed (17). 6sense email agents hand replies to reps by rule (23). Unify auto-pauses on out-of-office (6). Nex flags deals going dark (8).
8. **Outcomes feed back into targeting.** 6sense trains its qualification model on each customer's CRM outcomes (23), Salesloft learns action priority from outcomes (26), Lightfield stores sequence results by audience and angle (7), Monaco runs a reflection loop over transcripts into organisation memory (10), and AiSDR trains on its past campaigns (17).
9. **Consolidation is under way.** Zoom agreed to buy Common Room (13) and HubSpot agreed to buy Warmly (14) in mid-2026 (closing not confirmed at the time of research). Clarify bought Seam (1), Salesloft merged with Clari (26), ZoomInfo bought DoubleO.ai (21), and 1mind took over Drift customers (9). Signal and identity layers are being absorbed into suites.
10. **Agent-ready distribution.** MCP servers or APIs for other agents to call are now common: Clarify (1), Leadsforge (4 and 16), Warmly (14), Apollo (20), ZoomInfo (21), 6sense (23), Salesloft (26), Nex (8).

### How the best players promise and prove results

**What they promise.** The strongest claims are stated in the buyer's outcome unit, not in activity:
- Meetings per contacts reached: AiSDR "1 to 3 qualified meetings per 100 leads" (vendor claim, 17).
- Pipeline in money: Unify's Perplexity case study, $1.7M pipeline in three months with 75+ opportunities (vendor case study, 6).
- Revenue measured in the customer's own system: 1mind's HubSpot case study, influenced purchases up 25% "measured by HubSpot" (vendor case study, 9).
- Before and after on one metric: HubSpot's RevenueWell, meetings booked from 8% to 28% (vendor-reported on an earnings call, 27). Salesforce's own sales team, 130,000 untouched leads worked and 3,200 opportunities created in four months (vendor claim, 22).

**How they price around outcomes.** HubSpot charges $1 per lead the Prospecting Agent recommends and $0.50 per resolved conversation (27). Leadsforge charges only for verified results (4 and 16). AiSDR prices per person prospected (17). Rox sells usage and outcome units (15). Nex offers "pay for outcomes" on custom plans (8). Salesforce charges $0.10 per agent action (22). Seat-based pricing is giving way to credits and outcome meters, even among incumbents.

**How they prove it.** Proof quality varies widely:
- Strongest: audited filings (Salesforce, ZoomInfo, HubSpot investor materials) and results measured in the customer's own system (1mind).
- Medium: named case studies with numbers (Unify, HubSpot, Apollo's "2.3x meetings" for beta users).
- Weakest: commissioned studies (Salesloft's Forrester TEI, 26), logo walls without metrics (Reevo, 2), and early companies with no published results (Nex, 8; kōdlo, 5).

**Lesson for us.** The best players pick one outcome unit, show a before and after on it, and let the customer's own CRM be the referee. Demand AI should report qualified meetings and pipeline per 100 accounts released, measured in the client's CRM, and stop leading with activity counts.

### Top five changes to make to Demand AI

1. **Close the reply loop (Follow-up and response tracking).** Pull Dripify and email replies back into Demand AI, classify each one (positive, neutral, not now, wrong person, opt-out), stop or pause the sequence automatically, and make answering a positive reply the next best action with a same-day due time. Learned from Conversica (12), AiSDR (17), Unify (6), 6sense (23). Difficulty: Medium. Classification is a small LLM task; the work is getting reply data out of Dripify and inboxes.
2. **Find the right person before an account is released (Qualified lead identification).** Add an enrichment step that looks for a verified primary-persona contact through two or three providers in order, and block release when only a secondary contact or an unverified email exists. Learned from Leadsforge (4 and 16), Apollo (20), ZoomInfo (21), Unify (6). Difficulty: Medium, mostly vendor contracts and cost control.
3. **Stack signals and show the reason (Lead scoring).** Score on the count, type and recency of distinct signals, add at least one live intent source for the 61% of qualified accounts with no signal (website visits or a co-op feed), and show which signals moved each score. Learned from Unify (6), 6sense (23), Bombora (24), Warmly (14), HubSpot (27). Difficulty: Low for stacking and reasons, Medium to High for a new intent source.
4. **Turn Next Best Campaign into a play with mapped steps (NBA and NBC, Content mapping).** Define each campaign as trigger, audience, persona, steps and timings, with one asset per persona per step, short signal-led first touches, and long-form content held until someone engages. Generate each step's message at send time from the account's latest signal and log the result per step, angle and asset. Learned from Unify plays (6), Salesloft Cadence (26), Lightfield (7), Workvivo Journeys (11), AiSDR (17). Difficulty: Low; the rules engine already has the segment library.
5. **Write outcomes back and learn from them (Deal).** Write every release, reply, meeting and opportunity to the client's CRM, add the meeting as a stage with its own next best action, and recalibrate tier thresholds and signal weights against which accounts produced meetings. Report results in one outcome unit (meetings and pipeline per 100 released accounts). Learned from Clarify (1), Rox (15), 6sense (23), Salesloft (26), HubSpot (27). Difficulty: Medium; CRM write-back needs one integration per CRM.

## AI-native vs incumbent

### What the AI-native players do differently

- **Capture instead of entry.** Clarify, Reevo, Lightfield, Rox and Monaco build the record from email, calendar and calls. Incumbent CRMs still depend on reps typing.
- **Act, not just suggest.** AiSDR, Conversica, 1mind and Monaco run the work end to end and hand off to people at defined points. Incumbent tools mostly rank and suggest, although Agentforce and HubSpot's agents are closing this gap fast.
- **Price on usage and outcomes.** Credits, per-result and per-outcome pricing (Leadsforge, AiSDR, Rox, Nex, Monaco) instead of seats. HubSpot has already copied this with $1 per recommended lead.
- **Speed to value.** A free tier and a script install (Warmly), a day-one TAM (Monaco), a selling profile from a URL (HubSpot copied this too), an agent built on the first call (Nex).
- **Rent data, compete on workflow.** Waterfalls over many providers rather than one owned dataset.
- **Publish engineering.** Monaco (memory design), Aurasell (fine-tuned 8B model), nRev (generator plus verifier) and Rox (architecture docs) explain how they work. Incumbents rarely do.
- **Bundle people.** AiSDR's managed service, Monaco's forward-deployed AE and nRev's support hours sell an outcome, not a tool.

### Where incumbents still win

- **Data.** ZoomInfo's graph and research team, Apollo's contributor network, Bombora's exclusive co-op and 6sense's intent network are hard to copy. Clarify had to buy Seam to get signals.
- **Distribution.** HubSpot switched agents on for almost 17,000 Prospecting Agent customers inside existing seats. Salesforce reported Agentforce ARR above $1.5bn (on a widened definition).
- **Trust and governance.** Salesforce's Trust Layer, ZoomInfo's compliance certifications and permission models matter most in enterprise. The Drift token breach at Salesloft shows the other side: a wider integration surface carries more risk.
- **Integrations and system of record.** Lightfield, Aurasell and Nex all offer sync or overlay modes, which concedes that incumbents keep the record during any transition.

### Gaps a new entrant like us can exploit

1. **Vertical signals the horizontal vendors do not hold.** Demand AI already scores on installed-base end-of-support dates, service renewals, capital projects and turnaround timing for process plants. None of the 25 companies researched is built around industrial installed-base or project signals. Inference: these signals come from the client's own service and account records, not from intent co-ops, which gives us a data position the horizontal players cannot buy.
2. **Long-cycle, high-ticket selling.** Most AI SDRs optimise for volume and meetings per 100 contacts. Only kōdlo targets high-ticket B2B, and it is pre-traction. A tool that tracks one opportunity over a 9 to 18 month cycle, with the buying committee mapped, fits our clients better than a volume engine.
3. **Transparent, auditable scoring.** HubSpot, 6sense, Salesloft and ZoomInfo do not publish how scores work. Our rules engine can show every weight, decay and knock-out, which matters to engineering buyers and to clients who need to defend the list.
4. **Intent is thinning at the top of the funnel.** Bombora reports broad publisher traffic down 25% year on year as AI search answers early questions. Inference: first-party and late-stage signals (inquiries, installed base, service records, meetings) will count for more than third-party reading, which plays to our signal set.
5. **Regional coverage.** Inference, not verified: the large contact databases are strongest in North America and Europe. Coverage of plant engineers in South-East Asia is likely thinner, which is consistent with our primary-persona coverage gap. A partner-plus-research approach (kōdlo-style cited research) may beat a bigger database here.
6. **Work on top of what clients already use.** Clients keep their CRM, Dripify and inboxes. An overlay that reads replies and writes outcomes back, like nRev and Nex, is easier to adopt than a CRM replacement.
